About the Stack Player

What this is

The Stack Player lets anyone try an xTalk stack in a web browser, with nothing to install. It is part of OXT-Beyond, a free and open-source xTalk environment in the OpenXTalk family. Stacks from OpenXTalk and other compatible engines open here.

  • The desktop's engine, in the browserOXT-Beyond's engine is compiled to WebAssembly, so a stack runs its own scripts and behaves as it does on a computer.
  • Open, play, keepOpen a stack file, a folder or a .zip. It opens in the page, and stays in your stacks in this browser for next time.
  • Take your changes with youDownload copy saves the stack as it is now, with whatever it or you changed, as a file that OpenXTalk and other compatible engines open.

It is a beta. Most modern stacks work; a few things no browser allows, and a few are not done yet. The other pages say which, in plain English.

What works

These work as they do on the desktop.

    What works differently

    These work, but a browser does them its own way.

      What doesn't work

      Some things a browser never lets a web page do. A stack that uses them still opens, and those commands fail as they would on a computer that can't do them.

        Your files

        Everything happens inside this browser tab.

        • Nothing is uploadedThe stacks you open are copied into this browser's storage for this site, not sent to a server.
        • Your stacks stay until you remove themYour stacks, and what they save, are kept for your next visit in this browser. Remove one with the bin on its card, or clear this site's data in your browser.
        • A stack is a programA stack runs its own scripts. Here it can't touch your computer's files, but it can do what any web page can: show things, save into this site's storage, read what other stacks saved here, and make web requests. Open stacks you trust, as you would any program.
