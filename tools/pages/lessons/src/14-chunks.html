Asking for a piece

Most languages make you count positions and cut strings with functions. xTalk lets you ask for a piece of text in words: word 2 of, char 1 to 3 of, the last line of. These are called chunk expressions, and they are one of the reasons people love the language.

The four chunks

ChunkA piece of text separated by
charnothing: one character (character also works)
wordspaces, tabs or new lines (and a quoted phrase counts as one word)
itemcommas, or the itemDelimiter
linenew lines, or the lineDelimiter

Counting from the end

A negative number counts from the end: -1 is the last, -2 the one before. the last and the first read even better when that is what you mean.

As the last line shows, chunks go inside chunks: a character of a word of a line of a field is a perfectly good thing to ask for.

Other separators

Not every list uses commas. Set the itemDelimiter to any character to split on it. Like caseSensitive, it goes back to a comma when the handler ends. (The lineDelimiter works the same way for lines.)

Words and punctuation

A word is anything between spaces, punctuation and all. When you want the words a person would count, use trueWord, which knows about punctuation (and about languages without spaces).

Asking for too much

Asking for a chunk that is not there is not an error: you just get empty. That makes loops and checks simple.

A list that ends with its separator has no empty item at the end: "a,b," has 2 items, not 3. That way a list built with put tItem & comma after tList counts right.
