Reading the clock

xTalk has functions for now: the date, the time, and the same in long and abbreviated forms. the seconds counts seconds since the start of 1970, and the milliseconds thousandths of a second, which are handy for timing things.

the date uses the American order, month first, so that scripts always read and write it the same way, wherever they run. the system date gives it in the order of the user's own settings, for showing to people.

Changing a date's form: convert

convert turns a date or time from any form xTalk understands into another: short date, long date, abbreviated date, seconds, internet date or dateItems. It changes the variable in place.

Taking a date apart: dateItems

In dateItems form a date is seven items: year, month, day, hour, minute, second, and the day of the week (1 is Sunday). It is the form to use for arithmetic:

1 March 2024 was day 6 of its week, a Friday.

Adding days and months

To move a date, add to an item of its dateItems and convert it back. You need not worry about the ends of months or years: a day 32 or a month 13 rolls over into the next one.

Adding a month to 31 January gives "31 February", which rolls over into March. Decide what your program means by "a month later" before you add one.
