Who talks to whom

Tor hides who talks to whom: a connection hops through three computers, and none of them knows both ends. An onion service goes further, a program reachable only inside Tor at an onion address, whose own location stays hidden too. OnionXT, the xTalk Suite's Tor library, is written in xTalk and drives a tor program running beside your app (Tor Browser has one); its handlers begin with ox.

Everything in this lesson works without Tor, so every example runs here. Dialling and running services need Tor itself, and the last section says how they go.

oxVersion gives OnionXT's version, and Tor's after it when Tor is running. oxTransportInfo says what works here: with SodiumXT loaded, addresses can be made and checked offline. oxIsReady is true only once Tor is running and connected.

Onion addresses

An onion address is 56 letters and digits, then .onion. Nobody registers it: it is made from a key, and has a version and a checksum built in. oxIsValidAddress checks all of it, so a typo is caught before you try to connect. The address is a public key, 32 bytes, written in base32 (the letters a to z and the digits 2 to 7) with the checksum and version after it. oxPublicKeyFromAddress takes the key out, and oxAddressFromPublicKey turns a key back into its address. An address of your own

Your app can have an address of its own, the same every time it starts. It comes from a seed, 32 secret bytes: SodiumXT's sxSignKeypairFromSeed makes a key pair from it, oxAddressFromPublicKey turns the public key into the address without any need for Tor, and oxCreateServiceFromSeed later asks Tor to run it. Dialling and serving

To reach an address, dial it through Tor. oxDial takes a host (an onion address, or an ordinary name) and a port, and gives a stream handle at once; Tor then connects, and the handler you named with oxSetStreamCallback hears "open", then "data" as it arrives, or "error". oxWrite sends, oxStreamState says how a stream is, and oxCloseStream ends it. An onion service lets others dial you: after oxConnectControl reaches Tor's control port, oxCreateService makes a new address each time and oxCreateServiceFromSeed keeps one, and Tor forwards each visitor to a port on this computer. oxShutdown closes everything when your app quits.

Tor protects who talks to whom, not what you say: send only what you would be happy for a stranger to read, unless you also encrypt it, as SodiumXT does (Cryptography with SodiumXT). Learn OnionXT (Help > Examples) and the Dictionary have every handler, and onion-httpd, which comes with OnionXT, serves web pages at an onion address.
