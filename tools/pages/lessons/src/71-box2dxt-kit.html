Physics for the controls of a card

Box2D's own handlers (Physics with Box2Dxt) work in metres, and leave the drawing to you. The Box2Dxt Kit, a library that comes with Box2Dxt, does the rest: it works in pixels, screen coordinates and degrees, gives the controls of a card bodies, and moves each control to where its body is, 60 times a second. Its handlers begin with b2k. Beyond physics it reads the keyboard, plays sprite animations, runs a platform-game player, scrolls a camera and makes sounds; this lesson keeps to the physics.

On a card of your own, these few lines are a whole toy: everything that falls, lands, rolls and piles up, and the mouse can grab and throw it.

b2kQuickStart makes a world with gravity, puts invisible walls round the card's edges, and starts the loop that steps the world and moves the controls. b2kSpawnBall and b2kSpawnBox make filled graphics with bodies, given their middle and size in pixels and a colour. b2kStop stops the loop when the card closes.

A step at a time

b2kQuickStart is b2kSetup (a new world), b2kAddWalls and b2kStart (the loop). To see exactly what happens, a script can b2kPause the loop and move it on itself with b2kStepOnce, one sixtieth of a second each, which is how the examples here run. b2kPosition says where a body's middle is, in pixels, and b2kTeardown ends it all:

The walls run along the card's edges, so the ball, 60 pixels across, rests with its middle 30 pixels above the bottom of the card, which is 360 high. Underneath, the Kit still works in metres: b2kSetScale says how many pixels make a metre (40, to begin with), and b2kSetOrigin where on the card the world's 0,0 is.

Your own controls

A body can belong to any control you drag onto a card. b2kAddBox gives a control a box the size of its rectangle, b2kAddBall a circle, and b2kAddStatic a body that never moves, for floors and shelves. After that the Kit moves the control. Build a card:

  1. In Edit mode, drag a Rectangle Graphic to the left of the card, wide and thin, for a shelf. Name it Shelf in the Property Inspector, tick Opaque and give it a grey fill.
  2. Drag a smaller Rectangle Graphic above the shelf, name it Crate, and give it an orange fill.
  3. Drag an Oval Graphic to the right, high up, name it Ball, and give it a teal fill.
  4. Drag a Standard Button to the top right and name it Drop.
create graphic "Shelf"
set the opaque of it to true
set the backgroundColor of it to "#9eabbd"
set the rect of it to 40,200,240,216
create graphic "Crate"
set the opaque of it to true
set the backgroundColor of it to "#ff9b3d"
set the rect of it to 100,60,160,110
create graphic "Ball"
set the style of it to "oval"
set the opaque of it to true
set the backgroundColor of it to "#2bb5a5"
set the rect of it to 330,40,380,90
create button "Drop"
set the rect of it to 380,10,462,33

Give the button this script, then click it in Run mode. The crate falls onto the shelf and the ball to the floor, live:

The loop runs on a timer, so the script ends at once and the card stays live: you can click other buttons while things fall. A button or a field can have a body too, and still works when clicked; it moves without turning, so its text stays upright.

Pushes, kicks and hinges

There are three ways to make a body go, and they feel different. b2kSetVelocity sets its speed outright; b2kPush adds to its speed, the same for a pebble as a boulder; and b2kImpulse kicks it, so a heavy body moves less. Speeds are in pixels a second, and a negative y is up. b2kHinge pins a control to the world, or to another control, at a point, for doors, pendulums and wheels:

When things touch

A game needs to know when a ball reaches the goal or a player lands on a spike. Tell the Kit which object should hear about it with b2kContactTarget, and after each frame it sends that object b2kContact, with the two controls that began touching (a control is empty for a wall or the ground). Try it yourself

Build a tower of five boxes, 40 pixels square, one on another in the middle of the floor, let it settle, then fire a heavy ball at it from the left with b2kSetVelocity. How many boxes are still higher than the floor's first row two seconds later?
