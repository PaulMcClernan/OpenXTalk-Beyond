The world's time zones

It is noon in London when it is nine at night in Tokyo, and the difference changes twice a year in many places, on dates each country chooses. The Time Zone library carries the tz database, the list of every zone's rules that phones and computers everywhere use, so a program can know the time anywhere. Each zone has a name, a region and its biggest city: Europe/Paris, America/New_York. TimeZones lists them:

Use these names, not abbreviations like EST or CET: an abbreviation can mean different zones in different countries, and does not say whether summer time applies.

Offsets from UTC

Every zone's clock is a number of hours ahead of or behind UTC, the world's reference time, which is the time in London in winter. the seconds counts in UTC, the same at that moment everywhere. FromUniversalTime takes a moment in UTC seconds and a zone, and gives that zone's clock as seconds, so the difference is the zone's offset:

An offset is not always whole hours: India is five and a half hours ahead, Nepal five and three quarters. Sydney is eleven ahead in January because it is summer there.

Summer time

Many zones put their clocks forward an hour in summer, and each does it on its own dates; the south's summer is the north's winter. The same zone at two moments half a year apart:

London and New York go forward in July, Sydney goes back, and Japan has no summer time at all. The library knows the rules for past years too, as they were, so old dates come out right.

A zone's clock

The seconds FromUniversalTime gives are a clock reading: read as if they were UTC, they show that zone's time. A day is 86400 seconds and an hour 3600, so mod and div take the hours and minutes out, and comparing whole days says whether the zone is already in the next day, or still in the day before:

Do not convert the seconds FromUniversalTime gives. convert reads seconds as UTC and turns them into your computer's time, so it adds your own zone's offset on top of the other zone's. On a computer set to UTC the answer looks right; on your users' computers it is hours out. Take the clock out with mod and div, as here.

A meeting across zones

ToUniversalTime goes the other way: from a zone's clock reading, as seconds, to the moment in UTC. A clock reading as seconds is the day's midnight, counted as if in UTC, plus the hours and minutes. A call set for nine in the morning in New York on 15 June 2026, from Paris and Tokyo:

Store moments in UTC seconds, as the seconds gives them, and turn them into a zone's clock only to show them. Then a list of events from people in many zones sorts into the right order, and summer time never makes one an hour out.

Twice a year a clock that changes for summer skips an hour or repeats one, so some readings, such as 2:30 in the morning on the day the clocks go forward, never happen in that zone, and others happen twice. Avoid setting things for those hours.
