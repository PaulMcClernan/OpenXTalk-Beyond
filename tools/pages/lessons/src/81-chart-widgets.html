A line graph

A column of numbers says little at a glance; a line that climbs and falls says it at once. The Line Graph widget draws one or more lines from its graphData: a line of text for each point along the bottom, its label first and then a value for each line of the graph, separated by commas. It works out the scale and labels its axes itself:

Here the first value of each day is Madrid's temperature and the second Oslo's. graphColors gives each line its colour, a line of text each, as red, green, blue and opacity; markerStyles gives each its dots, a line each too, such as filled circle, square or the name of an icon, or none at all when it is empty. It starts as filled circle, filled square and filled diamond, which is why Oslo has squares.

The side runs from the smallest value to the largest, in as many steps as there are points along the bottom, and each label is rounded to a whole number: seven days make six steps from 8 to 31, so the labels are 8, 12, 16, 20 and so on.

The data can come from a calculation as well as a list. Savings of 1000 that grow by 5% a year are worth 1000 times 1.05 to the power of the number of years, 1000 * 1.05 ^ tYear. Here they are every second year for twenty years, with one point marked by hilitedCoordinates, which draws a dot there and dashed lines to the axes:

Line 6 of the data is year 10, because the years start at 0 and go up in twos. After ten years the money has grown by more than half, and after twenty it has more than doubled: the line curves up because each year's growth is on a bigger sum.

A pie chart

A Pie Chart shows how a whole is shared out. Build a card with one:

  1. Make a new stack, or go to an empty card. In Edit mode, drag a Pie Chart from the widgets of the Tools palette to the left of the card, make it about 260 pixels square, and name it Budget.
  2. Drag a classic Field to the right of it and name it Info.
create widget "Budget" as "com.livecode.widget.piechart"
set the rect of it to 20,40,280,300
create field "Info"
set the rect of it to 300,40,460,100

A clock

The Clock widget is a clock face with hands that move, and a darker face at night, from eight in the evening until six in the morning. It shows the computer's own time unless its timeZone is set, to the seconds that zone is ahead of UTC. The Time Zone library (Time zones) knows that number for every city, summer time included, so a wall of clocks for the world takes a few lines:

A real world clock would use the seconds for the moment, and set the offsets again now and then, since they change with summer time. The hands move by themselves; the page shows no picture of them because they would be at a different time each time it was checked.

A calendar

The Calendar widget shows a month, with arrows to go to the one before or after, and lets people choose a day with a click, which sends dateChanged with that day. Its selectedDate is the chosen day, written year, month and day, 2024-02-14, which sorts and compares correctly as text and is how databases write dates too. Setting it shows that day's month:

A handler of dateChanged in the Calendar's script gets the day as its parameter: on dateChanged pDate. To turn that into one of xTalk's own forms of date, replace the dashes with commas and convert it from dateItems (Dates and times).
