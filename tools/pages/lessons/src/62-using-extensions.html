What an extension is

An extension adds to xTalk without changing the engine. OXT-Beyond comes with dozens, and loads every one in its Extensions folder as the IDE starts, so they are ready before your first line. They come in three kinds:

  • Libraries add commands and functions: time zones, QR codes, comparing text, a web server, signing in with OAuth2. Once loaded, you call them just as you call put or the date.
  • Widgets are new kinds of control, such as a switch, a tree view or a chart. They sit in the Tools palette under the classic controls, and you drag them onto a card the same way.
  • The xTalk Suite is OXT-Beyond's own family of libraries, each a thin layer over a well-known native library: SodiumXT for cryptography, CoinXT for Bitcoin and Ethereum keys, NostrXT for the Nostr network, ENet, dataChannelXT and TorrentXT for peer-to-peer networking, OnionXT for Tor, and Box2Dxt for physics.

Some extensions are written in xTalk Builder (Extensions in xTalk Builder) and compiled; others, the script libraries, are plain xTalk in a stack. To a script that calls them, the two look the same. The lessons after this one take them a few at a time, and every example in them runs on the engine with the extension it needs.

Finding them

Choose Tools > Extension Manager to see what is installed: each library and widget with its name, version and author. Its Install extension button adds one that someone gives you as a package, a .lce file, and the IDE loads it at once. Every command, function, property and message an extension adds has its page in the Dictionary (Help > Dictionary), as the language's own words do.

A script can ask what is loaded. the loadedExtensions lists the extensions written in xTalk Builder, a line each, by their id: a name in reverse-domain form, such as com.livecode.library.json, that no two extensions share:

A widget from the Tools palette

The widgets are at the bottom of the Tools palette, below the classic controls. Build a card with one:

  1. Make a new stack, or go to an empty card. In Edit mode, scroll the Tools palette down to its widgets and drag a Switch Button onto the top left of the card.
  2. Double-click it to open the Property Inspector, and name it Dark mode. Its other sections are the switch's own properties: whether it is on, its colours and its theme.
create widget "Dark mode" as "com.livecode.widget.switchbutton"
set the rect of it to 40,40,100,72

Extensions of your own

An extension you write, or download as source, is compiled in the Extension Builder (Tools > Extension Builder), which can also install it. A script can load a compiled module itself with load extension from file and drop it with unload extension, as Extensions in xTalk Builder shows. A standalone carries only the extensions ticked on the Inclusions pane of its settings (Shipping your program), so tick every one your program uses.
