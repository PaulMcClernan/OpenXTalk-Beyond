Straight from one program to another

WebRTC lets two programs talk directly, peer to peer, even when both sit behind home routers, with no server in the middle carrying their messages. Browsers use it for video calls. The xTalk Suite's dataChannelXT uses its data channels, through libdatachannel, to send text and bytes; its handlers begin with dc, and its helpers, such as dcStateName, come with it.

The examples run both peers in one script on one computer, so each waits a moment for the other with wait until, never more than five seconds. In an app each peer is a different program, and events tell it when something happens.

A peer

A peer is your end of one connection. dcCreatePeer makes one; its text names ICE servers, helpers that find a way through routers (empty is enough on one computer, and a line such as stun:stun.l.google.com:19302 helps across the internet). Its state and how far it has got finding its addresses are numbers, which dcStateName and dcGatheringName say in words. dcFreePeer frees it, and its handle stops working:

Offer and answer

Before two peers can talk they swap two short texts: an offer and an answer. Each lists the addresses its peer can be reached at, its candidates. Making a channel makes a peer write its offer, which is complete when dcGatheringState is 2; dcLocalDescription gives the text. Connecting the two

With both peers in one script, the texts go straight across: A writes its offer, B takes it with dcSetRemoteDescription and writes its answer, and A takes B's answer the same way. The peers then try each other's addresses, and the channel opens:

Messages are events

dcSendText sends text on an open channel, and dcSendData sends bytes, up to 60000 a message (-4 means too big). On the other side each message is an event: dcPoll gives every event since the last poll, oldest first, as an array, each with a "name", the "peer" and "channel" it is about, and more. A message is a "dcMessage" event, with its text under "text" and bytes under "payload". In an app, poll on a timer, or let dcStartPolling do it:

Channels your way

dcCreateChannelEx makes a channel with its options: a protocol name; unordered, so a message need not wait for the one before it; retries and a time limit in milliseconds (0 retries means a lost message stays lost, fine for game moves where only the newest counts, and -1 means no limit); and negotiated with a stream id, so both sides make the channel themselves and nothing is announced:

Closing down

dcCloseChannel closes a channel, and both ends get a "dcChannelClosed" event. dcClosePeer hangs up the whole connection, and the other side sees its state go to closed. dcFreePeer then frees the peer and its channels:

Never call dcCleanup while another stack might use dataChannelXT: it ends every peer in OXT-Beyond, not just yours. Free your own peers with dcFreePeer.

Across the internet, two peers also need a way to swap their offer and answer, and sometimes a TURN server to relay for them when no direct route exists. Learn dataChannelXT (Help > Examples) and the Dictionary say more.

Try it yourself

Write a command connectPair that makes two peers and a channel, connects them and returns the three handles as items (A, B, channel), or empty after five seconds without an open channel. Use it to send three numbered lines from A to B, and put what B gets.
