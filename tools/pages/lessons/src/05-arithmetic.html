The operators

xTalk uses the symbols you know from maths, with * for times, / for divide and ^ for "to the power of".

Whole numbers: div and mod

div divides and drops the part left over. mod gives only what is left over. They are more useful than they look: mod 2 tells you whether a number is even, and the pair turns seconds into minutes and seconds.

With a negative number, div rounds towards zero and mod takes the sign of the number divided: -7 div 2 is -3 and -7 mod 2 is -1.

Which goes first

As in maths, ^ comes first, then *, /, div and mod, then + and -. Operators of the same kind go from left to right. Brackets change the order, and make a long sum easier to read even when they do not.

Decimals

Division gives decimals when it needs them. xTalk keeps many digits, but shows at most six decimal places unless you ask for more.

Many languages say 0.1 + 0.2 is 0.30000000000000004, and that it is not equal to 0.3. xTalk compares numbers the way people do, as HyperCard did, so the sum is 0.3.

Dividing by zero

A number divided by zero has no answer, so xTalk stops with an error rather than make one up.

When a value might be zero, check it first with if (the subject of Making decisions).
