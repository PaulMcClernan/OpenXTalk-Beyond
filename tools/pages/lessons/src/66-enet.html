Messages for games

A socket (Sockets) carries a stream of bytes. A game wants something else: short packets that arrive whole, some of which must arrive (a chat line, a move) and some of which may be dropped if a newer one is coming (where a player is now). ENet gives exactly that, over UDP, and the xTalk Suite's enetxt brings it to xTalk with handlers that begin with en. Its helpers, such as enStateName and enFormatBytes, come with it.

ENet never calls your scripts by itself. You call enPoll again and again, on a timer in an app, and each call hands back what has happened since the last: a peer connected, a packet arrived, a peer left. Here the server and the client are in one script on one computer, talking through 127.0.0.1, and a loop polls both.

Hosts and holds

enInitialize takes a hold on the library before any other call, and enDeinitialize gives it back, once for each hold. A host is your end of the line: enHostCreateServer makes one that listens, given the address to listen on, a port (0 picks any free one), how many peers may join, how many channels each has, and speed limits in bytes a second (0 for none). enHostStatus says where it listens:

A closed host's handle is retired: asking about it gives empty, never another host's answer.

Connecting

A client host connects to a server. enConnect gives a peer handle at once, but the connection is real only when an event says so. Each event that enPoll returns is an array with a "name": enetConnect, enetReceive or enetDisconnect. Text and bytes

Once connected, enSendText sends text on a channel, and the other side gets an enetReceive event whose "payload" is the bytes: textDecode turns them back into text. enSend sends any bytes, up to 60000 a packet, which is how games keep their messages small. Both return 0 when the packet is queued, and a negative number when not (-4: too big). Packets leave at the next poll, or at once with enFlush:

Channels and delivery

A connection can have several channels, numbered from 0, and a late packet on one never holds up another. Each send also has a delivery flag:

  • 0, reliable: resent until it arrives, in order. For chat and moves.
  • 1, unreliable: in order, but it may be dropped. For where a player is now: a newer one comes soon.
  • 2: it may be dropped, or arrive out of order.

Health, and goodbye

enPeerStatus tells a game how a connection is: its "state", the round trip in milliseconds ("rtt"), packets lost, and bytes sent and received, which enFormatBytes writes for people. enSetPeerTimeout decides when a quiet peer counts as gone. enDisconnect ends a connection politely: queued packets go first, then both sides get an enetDisconnect event, and the peer handle is retired, so a send to it is refused:
